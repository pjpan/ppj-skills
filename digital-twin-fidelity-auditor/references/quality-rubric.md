# Replication Quality Rubric

Use this file when Q3–Q7 need deeper assessment.

## Individual Specificity

The central question is whether target-specific signal exceeds archetype signal.

Useful checks:
- remove the person's name;
- remove iconic phrases;
- remove identity-revealing biography;
- compare against generic archetype agents;
- test decisions driven by unusual personal trade-offs.

High specificity requires evidence that the system remains target-like after obvious identity cues are removed.

## Generalization

Test multiple levels:

1. seen domain / unseen case;
2. unseen combination of familiar factors;
3. value-conflict cases;
4. resource/risk extremes;
5. unseen domain when the claimed scope permits.

Generalization should not require identical surface details.

## Consistency

Distinguish:
- horizontal consistency across topics;
- longitudinal consistency across long interactions;
- cross-layer consistency between represented values, interpretation, prediction, and choice;
- context-sensitive change.

Rigid sameness is not necessarily fidelity.

## Temporal Adaptation

Test:
- historical-state reconstruction;
- no future-information leakage;
- versioned memory/beliefs;
- update latency;
- correction propagation;
- ability to distinguish “past person” from “current person”.

For living users, new real behavior should have a controlled update path.
For historical figures, future evidence must not contaminate earlier-state prediction.

## Calibration

A twin should know when its person-specific prediction is weak.

Evaluate:
- confidence versus actual similarity;
- evidence sufficiency;
- abstention;
- domain boundary;
- escalation;
- confidence under identity-poor cases.

Distinguish:
- model uncertainty;
- lack of person-specific evidence;
- genuine ambiguity in the person's own behavior.

The twin may be confident about facts while uncertain about what the person would choose.
